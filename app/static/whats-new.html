What’s new

Changes you can see on this site, newest first

27 November 2026

  • Stories. A new Stories page in the menu gathers the story pages and films in one place: Every run, Eighteen runs at Shikisai, Three laps found, the Redline V12 clip and the tour.
  • Redline · Daikoku Chase. A 28-second film of our two runs at Daikoku Chase in the same Aston Martin Vulcan, made in Blender from the recordings. Compass wins by 0.38 seconds. Watch it, with car sound or with commentary, or ride along in Chase.

26 November 2026

  • Redline V12. A new 16-second clip. Watch it.

25 November 2026

  • Follow the Every Run GT in Chase. Pick a lap and its car wears that lap’s colour. The other runs stay visible as simple ghosts. Choose Close and drag to see the GT from the side. Ride along at Shimanoyama.

23 November 2026

  • Every run, from one start. A new short story page with a 74-second film: all 299 laps we’ve finished, on 38 routes, set off from one point at the same moment, each on the path it really drove. Then the camera zooms into four laps at Shimanoyama. A note under the film says how it was made. Watch it.
  • Shikisai story wording. Canadian spelling (tire, not tyre, here and on How it works), and the notes on how the films and the announcer were made now say “I” where it was one person’s work. Read the story.

21 November 2026

  • Two films in the Shikisai story. Our recorded runs, rebuilt as cars in Blender: a 9-second roadside clip at Corner 2, and the whole 18-run race, 2 minutes 24 seconds with engine sound and brake lights. A short note says how they were made. Watch them.
  • Hear the Shikisai start called like a race. Under the full film there’s now a 45-second version of the opening with a race announcer, as Fish goes from fifth to first through Corner 2. It has English captions, and the story shows the exact prompts the voice was made from.

20 November 2026

  • Eighteen runs at Shikisai. A new story page: all 18 Shikisai Sprint runs we finished in the Aston Martin Vulcan, raced together as ghosts on a map seen from above, with a camera that follows the pack. Then the numbers: every lap time, who’s quicker on which quarter of the road, how we drive, and who’s the better driver overall. Read it.
  • Watch the places change in Replay. The cards beside the map always followed the race order, but they jumped instantly and, with several runs by one driver, all said the same name, so a pass was easy to miss. Now the cards slide into their new places and the place number lights up on the car that moved up. Each card starts with its lap time, so you can tell your own runs apart. Cards are one line each, so six fit beside the map; the car you follow gets the full details. Click a card to follow that car.

16 November 2026

  • Round wheels and readable names. Chase cars now have round wheels with hubs, and when two cars run side by side their names no longer print on top of each other: the second moves up a line.
  • A cleaner Chase camera. The car you follow is now solid and shaded, and the others are see-through ghosts, since each lap was driven at a different time. The road has a surface and centre marks, there’s a sky above the horizon, corner numbers stand beside the road (the corner the leader is in is lit), and a box in the corner shows speed, gear and the gap. Jumps no longer draw the road through the air: the flight shows as a dotted line, which also stops a stray line shooting off the top of the view.
  • Chase cars move like the real car. On Replay’s Chase camera, each car now sits the way the game recorded it: it leans out of corners, tips its nose on climbs and in the air, and turns against the road when it slides. Brake lights come on when the driver brakes. When a car is in the air it says so above it and a dashed line drops to the ground, and the height strip marks the followed car’s jumps.
  • Chase is now a camera in Replay. Next to Top, 3D and Follow, Replay has a Chase camera that rides behind the car you pick, with Behind, Close and High views and Height ×2 or ×4 to stretch the climbs. Everything else in Replay works with it: corner links, the line under the view, the side panel and Copy link, which now keeps the camera you were using. The height strip under the view shows in every camera. The separate test page is gone, and its old links open Replay on the Chase camera.

13 November 2026

  • Height posts in the Replay’s 3D view. The 3D view now draws the route again on the ground with posts up to the road, as on Analysis, so you can see how high each part runs. Heights are also stretched to suit the route, so a fairly flat circuit no longer looks completely flat.
  • Chase view (test). A new test page, opened from Replay with “Chase view (test)”. It follows one car from behind in 3D: Chase, Close or High camera, drag to look round, and a choice of car. The road is the followed car’s own line with posts down to the lowest road nearby, Height ×2 and ×4 stretch the climbs, and a strip underneath shows the height of the whole route with every car on it. The cars are simple wireframe boxes. It’s a test, so it may change or go away.

11 November 2026

  • Clearer word when an AI recap can't be written. A busy week could be too big for one AI request, and the Recap page said only "Report facts exceed the bounded request size". It now says, before you press anything, that the week is too big for one request and that nothing was charged. The limit for one request is also higher ($0.04 instead of $0.01), so weeks like 2 November fit again. Each recap still costs what it really costs, usually well under a cent, from the same $1 a month.

5 November 2026

  • Three laps got their finish back. Three laps looked as if they stopped short or ended back at the start, because a stray frame from the next run was mistaken for a rewind. Nothing was lost from the recording, and those laps now run to the line in Replay, Analysis and Traces & corners. Temple CC now shows its corners. Lap times, standings and race reports didn’t change. Read the full story, including what can’t be recovered.

4 November 2026

  • Corners on the Replay. When a race report says C7 or Section 3, you can now see where that is. Corner marks sit on the Replay map (the Corners button hides them), a line under the map says which corner the leader is in, and with two laps it adds who was quicker there and both minimum speeds. Each car’s card shows its corner too.
  • Reports link to the road. Click a section or a corner in a race report, Then vs now or Traces & corners, including C7 or Section 3 in the engineer’s review, and the Replay opens just before that spot. Press Play and it stops once the last car has left. Clicking a mark on the map does the same.
  • A cleaner Δ time chart. On Analysis, the Δ time line could shoot up or down by about a whole lap at its right-hand edge, because a lap’s last sample had already started the next lap’s clock. It now runs true to the line. CSV exports get the same correction. Lap times and race reports didn’t change.

30 October 2026

  • Milestones. Each week on Recap now lists its milestones: first laps on new routes, your 50th or 100th lap, personal bests in three or more sessions running, and the first time you got ahead of the other driver on a route. All measured; no AI involved.
  • Within reach. Each week on Recap now lists the records each of you is closest to taking back, and by how much. “Where the gap is” opens the race report for that pair, section by section. The AI column points at the closest one and writes lap times as minutes and seconds.
  • Pages tell you when there are new results. Leave Rivalry, Stats, Recap or any other results page open: within about a minute of a lap finishing or a report being written, a bar at the bottom says so, with a Refresh button. Nothing reloads by itself, so you never lose your place.

29 October 2026

  • Weekly recap. A new Recap page tells the story of each week: who drove, personal bests and how much they improved, records that changed hands, and how many records each of you held at the start and end of the week. An AI writes a short column about every finished week, the Monday after it ends, using only the measured numbers.
  • Then vs now. On Rivalry, pick a route and see how your best there compares with your first clean run, section by section. An AI engineer can review what you improved, where time is still to find, and what to practise next.

28 October 2026

  • Race reports stop failing the fact check. When the AI engineer mentioned the first 250 m of a lap (a distance the report really measures), the fact check rejected the whole review and you saw “did not pass the fact check”. That distance is now recognised.

26 October 2026

  • One report on Rivalry. Rivalry now shows just the race report for the two laps you pick. The review of a whole session (consistency, bests, where time went across its attempts, with optional AI priorities) has moved to Analysis, as “Session review” under the lap comparison: it follows the laps in the comparison tray.
  • Share a race report. While you look at two laps on Rivalry, the address in your browser names them, so you can copy it, bookmark it or send it. The strip above the report has a Copy link button too (also on Traces & corners). Opening the link goes straight to that report.
  • Menu tidied. Replay is in the menu, and so is this page. The old Rivalry page has left the menu; it is still linked at the bottom of Rivalry.
  • One pair, three views. Once you pick an A and a B lap, the same strip sits on top of the race report, Traces & corners and Replay: who, which route, both times, and one click to each of the other views for the same two laps. You can swap A and B, or go back to Rivalry. The links on Rivalry use the same three names.
  • The engineer’s review comes first. In the race report, the written review now sits right under the headline, in its own box, above the section-by-section breakdown. The notes about signing in no longer read like an error.

25 October 2026

  • Race report with an AI race engineer. For any A and B lap: where the time went, section by section, the corners that mattered, and a written review with each driver’s strengths, costs and one thing to practise. Every number in the review is checked against the measurements; a review that states anything else is not shown. Each review is written once and saved, so opening it again costs nothing.
  • Written for you on a lead change. When a route record changes hands, the review of the new record against the time it beat is written automatically, at most 20 a month.
  • Latest on Rivalry. The newest lead change, who leads how many routes, the closest gap and the latest improvements, each one click from its race report.
  • Straight from a row. Each contested route on Rivalry links to its two best laps: race report, traces and replay.
  • Sprints fixed. Corner priorities in the session debrief and the traces view now work for sprints whose recording ended at the finish line.

13–16 October 2026

  • Session debrief: a measured review of A’s whole session, with optional AI-picked priorities.
  • Replay: recorded laps raced against each other on the map.
  • Stats: twelve sections over everything recorded.
  • Rivalry: the new Rivalry page, best-lap rules, attempt counts (every started event, finished or given up), and Analysis hides empty sessions by default.
  • How it works: what the recorder does, its rules and its known faults.
